Safety Concern — Multiple Units Occupied

Extensive unpermitted construction has been performed at both 5636 and 5630 Temple City Blvd, including full kitchen and bathroom renovations, plumbing replacement, and electrical work — all without building permits. Multiple residential units are currently occupied by tenants living in structures that have never been inspected for code compliance.

Complaint Summary

This complaint documents systematic unpermitted construction across two multi-unit residential properties on Temple City Blvd. Public records confirm that 5636 Temple City Blvd has zero building permits on file in over 60 years, despite multiple units having been gut-renovated with new kitchens, bathrooms, plumbing, and finishes. Similarly, 5630 Temple City Blvd has no interior rehabilitation permits despite online real estate listings advertising "newly remodeled" units with "copper plumbing," "premium quartz countertops," and renovated bathrooms.

The evidence includes City of Temple City permit records showing no permits, real estate listing screenshots proving renovations were performed, and photographs of the renovated units. The work was performed across multiple units in a pattern that indicates a deliberate strategy to renovate without permits to avoid inspection costs and code compliance requirements.

Evidence — 5636 Temple City Blvd

Key Finding

The City of Temple City permit database shows zero building permits have ever been pulled for 5636 Temple City Blvd. Despite this, multiple units have been completely renovated with new kitchens, bathrooms, flooring, and plumbing — as proven by real estate listing photos and tenant photographs.

Unit B — "Newly Remodeled" (No Permit)

A Zillow listing for 5636 Temple City Blvd Unit B advertises the unit as having a "newly remodeled kitchen" with new cabinets, countertops, and appliances. Photographs from the listing show a complete gut renovation of the kitchen and bathroom. No building permit exists for this work.

Unit E — Renovated Interior (No Permit)

Unit E was leased in May 2025 and advertised as "newly remodeled." The unit contains new flooring, renovated bathroom, and updated kitchen. The plumbing in this unit has experienced chronic failures consistent with improper installation — the toilet has been non-functional for 11 months due to what appears to be an improperly installed drain line. No building permit exists for the renovation or plumbing work.

HVAC — Undersized Mini-Split (No Permit, No Load Calculation)

Mechanical Code Violation — Undersized HVAC Equipment

A single ductless mini-split was installed to serve the entire apartment without a mechanical permit, without a Title 24 energy compliance load calculation, and without the required CF1R/CF2R/CF3R compliance forms. The unit is dramatically undersized for the space it serves.

CF1R/CF2R/CF3R Forms None filed with HERS rater or building department

California Building Code requires a mechanical permit for HVAC installation (CBC §106.1). California Energy Code (Title 24, Part 6, §150.2(b)) requires a load calculation to properly size heating equipment. The installer must file CF1R (Certificate of Compliance), CF2R (Certificate of Installation), and CF3R (Certificate of Verification) forms. None of these requirements were met.

The result: both bedrooms in the apartment have zero heating source. The equipment is rated for roughly half the space it serves. A neighbor in the unit below independently confirmed that their apartment also has no working built-in heater — they resort to microwaving heating pads for warmth. This indicates a building-wide systemic failure, not an isolated issue.
